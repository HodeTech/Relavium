# ADR-0147: A system-started question is a sheet with one frame and a wait clock that no guard reads

## Context

The product asks the person to decide in seven places: the approval card (a file edit or a command), the agent's question, the continue checkpoint, the run gate, the budget gate, the MCP consent card and the exit guard. They share a placement ([ADR-0129](0129-the-approval-card-is-docked-bounded-and-says-only-what-matters.md) §1: docked in the composer's place, the transcript above) and a family of guards, and they differ in how they look: each draws its own rule or none, the approval's `Approval needed` rule is amber, the question's label is in its own place, the gate cards write their verbs in the third person, and the wait is told by a separate busy row (`Waiting for your answer <time> · Esc stops the turn`) that sits above the footer, costing a row the card's budget has to reserve.

The wait is also the one number a person looks at while a turn is blocked on them. It must be on the card, and it must never become an input to a safety decision: the guards of [ADR-0129](0129-the-approval-card-is-docked-bounded-and-says-only-what-matters.md) notes 9 to 12 (the dwell, the quiet gap, the typeahead window, the lockout) count from the first acknowledged frame and the last foreign key, because a parked gate that is an hour old must still make a person wait out its dwell.

Two measurements shape how the clock is built. **A pending approval already repaints the chat at 12.5 Hz**: the store's tick flushes whenever the turn's status is `running`, and a card that waits keeps it so, so today the busy row's spinner rewrites the live region about twelve times a second for as long as the person thinks (about 1.3 KB per tick in the alternate screen and 0.8 KB inline; 1.4 to 3.6 ms of rendering). Retiring the busy row saves that only if nothing animated remains and the tick is gated. And **a ticking field inside a snapshot defeats the measured-frame proof**: the acknowledgement compares snapshots by reference and restarts the dwell on every new one, so a clock digit inside a snapshot would restart the dwell every second and no `Ctrl+Y` would ever count, which is a liveness bug that invites someone to loosen the comparison to field equality, which fails open.

The MVP-11 plan proposes that every one of these is a **sheet** with one frame. The popup mechanism ([ADR-0148](0148-a-user-started-report-setting-or-flow-is-a-centred-popup.md)) is for what the user starts; a sheet is for what the system asks.

## Decision

**A question the system asks is a sheet: docked where the prompt is, the conversation visible above it, one frame, a clock in its rule that is not in any snapshot, and not one guard changed.**

### 1. The frame

1. **A heavy rule** (`━━`) carries the label on the left and the **wait clock** on the right: `━━ Approval needed ━━━━━ waiting 0:41 ━━`. The rule is amber when the sheet asks for a decision and cyan for the agent's question. The sides are open (a tray, not a box), which costs no width, needs no extra work inline and leaves a screen reader no border glyph to read on every line.
2. **A title line:** what it is on the left, the numbers on the right (`+3 −1 · 214 bytes`).
3. **The body:** the diff, the command, the question and its options; line numbers and signs in the margin column, the text in the content column.
4. **Two key lines** with the real labels, coloured by the existing scheme (allow green, deny red, the rest cyan, `Esc` amber, words dim). `Esc`'s effect is always written ([ADR-0145](0145-the-cli-has-one-surface-language.md) §2).

The sheet takes the place of the prompt block (the prompt, its rule, the status line and the hint): **while a sheet is open the status line and the hint are not drawn**, because the sheet's key lines are the legend and its rule is the label; the provider, model and mode stay visible at every other time ([ADR-0146](0146-the-home-and-the-chat-share-one-calm-layout-and-the-first-run-has-no-dead-end.md) §2). It takes at most 12 rows at 80×24, and [ADR-0129](0129-the-approval-card-is-docked-bounded-and-says-only-what-matters.md) §2 is kept: three fifths of the rows, never fewer than eight transcript rows. The approval card, the agent's question, the continue checkpoint, the budget and gate cards, the MCP consent card and the exit guard (which replaces the prompt block on the Home, in the same place the prompt block has) all take the frame. **On the Home** the run gate, budget and MCP consent cards stay in the slot [ADR-0139](0139-a-workflow-run-is-hosted-inside-the-home.md) and [ADR-0140](0140-management-browsers-the-run-drill-down-and-the-durable-run-detail.md) give them (they take the body, and `Esc` leaves the card and decides nothing) and wear the same frame **inside** it. Docking them over the strip would change those two ADRs and the exit guard's measured frame; it is not done. A consent that belongs to a flow the user started (the MCP consent of a run launched from the `/run` popup) is a step of that popup wearing this frame ([ADR-0148](0148-a-user-started-report-setting-or-flow-is-a-centred-popup.md) §2), not a second sheet.

**Words that survive the new frame, word for word** (the pins that protect them are re-run on the new frames and never rewritten to pass): the agent's question keeps the rule label `Question from the agent · not an approval` and the host gutter `│ ` on every row of model-written text ([ADR-0132](0132-the-model-can-ask-the-user-a-structured-question.md)); the approval card keeps `(Allow is locked until you inspect the change)`, `(Allow is locked until you read the whole command)`, `Allow stays locked until you do.` and `⚠ Allow is locked until the required content has been inspected ([v]).` ([ADR-0129](0129-the-approval-card-is-docked-bounded-and-says-only-what-matters.md)).

**Without colour** the heavy rule is `=` and the thin rule `-`, so the two stay distinguishable; the label and the clock are words.

### 2. The wait clock is display state, and it is not in any snapshot

- **It replaces the busy row.** The row `Waiting for your answer <time> · Esc stops the turn` and its spinner retire; `Esc`'s effect is on the key line. The row budget is **re-derived in the sheets step from the live-region constants the code uses** (`LIVE_REGION_ROWS` and the card's row budget), not from this sentence; the invariant is the floor of eight transcript rows at 80×24 with the largest sheet, and the plan's arithmetic (a 23-row frame: the header, its blank row, nine transcript rows and a 12-row sheet) is the target it is checked against.
- **Not part of any snapshot.** The clock and any spinner are **not fields of the sheet's or the popup's snapshot**: they are a separate display value read by the one row that draws them, with a **reserved width** (the widest string it can take), so a tick never changes the frame's geometry. The identity is today's fields plus the new ones. For a **popup** the compared object is the channel's snapshot, and `getSnapshot()` returns the **same reference** until an identity field changes; for a **sheet** the compared object is the card's own review state, which a store flush per tick leaves unchanged. In both, the acknowledgement's reference comparison, and the dwell it restarts, are not disturbed by a tick. A test runs a thousand ticks and shows that the acknowledgement stays valid and `shown` was called once; a mutation that puts the elapsed text in a snapshot field must fail it.
- **It never feeds a guard.** Every guard keeps its existing origin: the dwell counts from the first frame that was measured and flushed, never from publication; the quiet gap from the last foreign key. A gate parked an hour ago shows `waiting 1h 07m` and still waits out its dwell from its first acknowledged frame. A test shifts the display clock and shows that no guard outcome changes.
- **The turn's time still excludes the wait** ([ADR-0129](0129-the-approval-card-is-docked-bounded-and-says-only-what-matters.md) §6: `1m02s · 3m56s waiting for you`). No event or history field carries the wait.
- **Refresh** *(refinement of OD-12)*. The clock is **derived at render from the current time, quantised to the unit it shows**, and the chat hosts get **no timer of their own**: the store's existing tick is **gated** so that it flushes only when something changed or the turn is running and not blocked on the person, and a single unref'd one-second timer, owned by the sheet host and cleared when the sheet closes **and** when its host unmounts (a late tick after either is a no-op), calls the flush while a sheet is open; the flush is skipped when the quantised clock text has not changed, so after the first minute a frame is drawn only at the minute boundary. In the alternate screen it shows seconds for the first minute and then **minutes only** (`waiting 2m`), aligned to the minute boundary, so the tick count falls from twelve a second to one a second and then to one a minute (the plan's "every ten seconds after a minute" would show seconds that look frozen and wake the loop five times in six for nothing). In the inline renderer the rule carries a static `waiting since HH:MM` and **nothing repaints on a timer**: a rewrite of the live region every second is hostile to assistive tools and costly over SSH. The Home's run gate, budget and consent cards have no store tick; each owns one unref'd timer that is cleared when the card closes. A regression test counts the bytes: with a sheet pending, N ticks write zero bytes inline and at most one frame a second in the alternate screen.
- **No new periodic repaint above 1 Hz** anywhere in the MVP (a popup's loading state included, [ADR-0148](0148-a-user-started-report-setting-or-flow-is-a-centred-popup.md) §5), because the renderer rewrites the whole frame for any changed cell (a one-digit change measured about 1.9 KB at 80×24) and synchronized output is atomic only where the terminal supports it.

### 3. What this ADR does not change

The answer chords (`Ctrl+Y`, `Ctrl+G` then Enter, `Ctrl+O` then Enter, `Ctrl+N`, `Ctrl+R`); every input guard and its constants; the measured-frame acknowledgement, which now also covers the new frame; the locks and the rule that a partial view earns no session-wide grant; the question's label and gutter; `Esc` and `Ctrl+C` never approving ([MVP-11 §5.5](../roadmap/phases/mvp-11.md#55-security-invariants-the-floor-that-does-not-move), rules 1 to 6, 11, 12, 15). The sheet's own inspection popup is [ADR-0148](0148-a-user-started-report-setting-or-flow-is-a-centred-popup.md)'s.

### Alternatives considered

- **A boxed sheet.** Rejected: borders cost two columns on every row, have to be drawn again inline and are read aloud line by line; an open tray costs none of that.
- **A centred modal for system questions.** Rejected, as [ADR-0129](0129-the-approval-card-is-docked-bounded-and-says-only-what-matters.md) rejected it: it hides the conversation the person is asked to decide about.
- **Keep the busy row.** Rejected: it costs a row, repeats the key line's `Esc` words, puts the one number that matters away from the question, and keeps the 12.5 Hz repaint.
- **A ticking clock inline.** Rejected: see §2.
- **The clock in the snapshot's identity.** Rejected: every tick would be a new frame, no answer would count for the frame the person saw, or the acknowledgement would be loosened to make it count. The clock is display state precisely so the proof does not move.
- **Show the wait as a countdown or a warning colour.** Not done: the wait is not an expiry; nothing times out.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Seven questions look like one family, and the person sees how long a turn has waited on them without a separate row.
- The busy row is gone, the budget is stated once from the code's own constants, and a waiting approval stops repainting the live region twelve times a second.
- The clock cannot weaken a guard, because nothing reads it and it is in no snapshot.

### Negative

- **Every card's layout changes**, and the approval-host suites (about 120 tests, several index the lines of the card), `Approval needed` (17 lines in 4 files) and `Esc stops the turn` change with it. Mitigation: the floor pins are re-run, not rewritten; each commit lists the pins it broke on purpose.
- **The 80×24 arithmetic is brittle** (a 12-row sheet leaves exactly nine transcript rows once the status line and hint are hidden). Mitigation: the surface matrix, derived from the code's constants, and a real-terminal check.
- **Gating the store's tick is a change to a hot path** every chat shares. Mitigation: the byte-count regression test and the existing store tests; the gate only ever removes flushes while the turn is blocked on the person.
- **The ASCII `=` rule** may look heavy in a plain terminal. Mitigation: it is the price of keeping the two rules apart without colour.
