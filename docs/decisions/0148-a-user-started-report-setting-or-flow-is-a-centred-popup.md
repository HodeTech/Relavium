## Context

A slash command that produces a report, a setting or a form has no surface of its own: it prints a note into the conversation (`/doctor`, `/cost`, `/mode`), replaces the screen with a picker (`/mode` on the bare Home, `/providers`), opens a docked sheet (`/agent`, `/permissions`) or writes a block under the Home strip (`/doctor` on the Home). Settings stay in the chat as lines that describe something that is not part of the conversation. The maintainer asked on 2026-11-24 that `/doctor` typed on the Home open a **popup** that `Esc` closes and that carries the controls that make sense there, that the same hold for every similar job, and that settings and configuration not come to the chat menu and stay there (D80, D81). A third ask: the approval's `[v]` preview should be a proper, polished popup.

[ADR-0137](0137-permissions-is-a-sheet-and-esc-steps-back-out-of-the-full-view.md) §1 had considered a popup for `/permissions` and rejected it because the renderer has no layering and the inline renderer could not draw one. That was a claim about the renderer, so the MVP's first step tested it with a prototype on the CLI's own Ink (below). The approval card's earlier rejection of a centred modal ([ADR-0129](0129-the-approval-card-is-docked-bounded-and-says-only-what-matters.md)) stands, and for the same reason: a modal over a decision the system is waiting on hides the conversation the person must read. That is why the **popup is for what the user started** and the **sheet is for what the system asks** ([ADR-0145](0145-the-cli-has-one-surface-language.md) §1).

The design review read the code the popup will land in and found three things the plan under-stated. **The existing prompt channel is one promise per step, and only some of today's overlays run on it**: `/models`, `/effort`, the chat's `/permissions` and `/agent` and the approval's full view are driven by their own reducers and stores, and the channel throws when a second flow begins. **"This overlay owns the keyboard" is written down in at least eight places**, including the gates that decide who holds the approval's custody; a popup that misses one of them leaves a card underneath it answerable. And **today's structural exclusion goes away**: a native card now replaces the body, so a card and another surface are never drawn together, whereas a popup is drawn over a live body, and the spike showed that a later sibling can cover a popup while the bounds check still passes.

## Decision

**A command that produces a report, a setting, a form or a picker opens a centred popup. It opens only on the user's command, between turns; it is closed by `Esc`; it asks nothing it has not said; it cannot be drawn over, or hide, a chord surface; and when it closes the conversation and the Home are exactly as they were, apart from what the command was for.**

### 1. Anatomy

Four parts, always in this order:

1. **The top edge** carries the command's name on the left and the **origin** on the right (`from Home`, `from chat`); a multi-step popup adds `step 2 of 3` there, so a glyph is never the only carrier of the step.
2. **The step rail**, for a command with more than one step, in the Home rail's visual language (`✓ Workflow ╌╌ ◉ Inputs ╌╌ ○ Confirm`). A one-step command has no rail. **A step with nothing to choose is skipped and has no rail node** (a workflow with no inputs, a scope with a single possible value). The `/models` second step (*Effort & scope*) is skipped only when its scope has one value as well, because where a change applies is itself a choice. `Esc` goes back one step and the rail shows where.
3. **The body:** a list, a form or a report. A filter line comes first when the list is long.
4. **The legend**, which always ends with the way back (`Esc back to chat`) and is the last thing truncated.

Every **report** popup also offers `[c] copy`, which puts its text on the clipboard as `/copy` does, because a closed report leaves nothing to re-read and a person pasting a doctor result into a bug report is the common case.

### 2. Opening, ownership and what a popup may cover

**The rules of opening.**

- **A popup opens only when no turn is running, no card or sheet is open and no two-step is armed.** A refused open says so in one notification. This is today's rule for overlays ([ADR-0137](0137-permissions-is-a-sheet-and-esc-steps-back-out-of-the-full-view.md) §1) and it is kept.
- **A system event never opens a popup, with one exception:** the first-run setup of [ADR-0146](0146-the-home-and-the-chat-share-one-calm-layout-and-the-first-run-has-no-dead-end.md) §6. It opens only when no conversation, turn, card, sheet, palette or screen exists and the composer is empty and idle; it is offered once per invocation; below 80×24 it waits and does not spend its offer; **and it applies the opening guard of §3** because it opens under the user's fingers.
- **A sheet over a popup: the sheet wins, and nothing is restored.** *(Refinement: the plan allowed a read-only report to be restored, which has no defined meaning for a popup that holds none of a chord, a secret or an armed step but is still in the middle of two steps.)* One rule for every popup: the sheet closes it **unanswered**, a secret buffer is cleared, its acknowledgement is invalidated and one notification says so. A popup is **never drawn over a sheet**, with one exception: the sheet's own inspection popup (§7). **The MCP consent of a run launched from `/run` is not a second exception to this rule**: it is a step of the popup the user started, requested by that flow and answered inside it as it is today, drawn in the sheet frame ([ADR-0147](0147-a-system-started-question-is-a-sheet-with-a-frame-and-a-wait-clock.md) §1), and it keeps the full scrub of untrusted text, the path refusal and the shared lockout; the plan's §5.5 rule 12, which concerns a system-started sheet arriving over a popup, is unchanged by it. A user action that would open a popup while a sheet is open is refused.
- **One owner of the chords at a time.** At most one surface owns `Ctrl+Y/N/G/O/R`. The existing cross-lockout pins and the recorded stale-card defect are the precedent. Tested with an injected clock in both orders.

**How a popup is built, and why it cannot hide a card.**

- **One shared pure frame with two body drivers.** The frame (the four parts, the placement and the acknowledgement) is one view. Its body is driven either by the **prompt channel** (the flows and the reports: a promise per step) or by a **pure reducer with host-owned state**, the pattern the palette uses (the pickers, the sheets and the review pages, which are not on the channel today and cannot be). The channel is **not** grown to hold both; the popup code lives in its own module directory (`apps/cli/src/popups/`) and the channel gains only an optional `popup` descriptor in its snapshot. The Home controller and the chat host route keys to a popup and open it, and hold no popup logic. A stack of depth two (§5) needs the reducer driver, because the channel refuses a second flow.
- **Placement.** The snapshot carries `placement: 'overlay' | 'replace'`. Today `prompt.active` means both "owns the keyboard" and "occupies the rows" (it hides the Home view, shrinks the transcript and makes the chat `display: none`). The two meanings are split into `ownsKeyboard` and `occupiesRows`, with frame tests for both placements in both hosts. `replace` is what the inline renderer uses (§4) and what a flow not yet converted keeps.
- **One overlay slot, the only absolutely positioned layer.** The popup is drawn in **one slot that is the last child of the root**, and it is the **only** absolutely positioned node in the CLI (a grep or lint test pins it), because z-order is tree order and the spike showed a later sibling covers the popup while the bounds check still passes. Notifications are never absolute.
- **One overlay-active predicate.** `overlayOwnsKeyboard()` is **one function over one record of flags**, and every place that today repeats the list derives from it: the Home's overlay and slot gates, the paste flags, the mouse-capture and wheel gates, the interrupt context, **the strip's refresh pause** (which gates a five-second synchronous database poll behind the popup), the stand-alone chat's equivalents, and **the custody predicates that decide whether the Home frame, a gate card or a review holds the approval's custody**. It is extracted as a pure refactor before the first popup is built, and the "a list was missed" test enumerates the custody predicates and the pause facts as well. Opening a popup is refused while a card exists, so a card holding custody under it can only arise through a race; the predicate then revokes that card's custody **and resets its acknowledgement and dwell origin** (today a card that regains custody keeps its old acknowledged frame and is answerable at once), so the card must be re-acknowledged on a new frame.
- **The measured-frame proof covers the popup, and the identity is today's plus the new.** The snapshot identity is today's field list (including `note` and `picker`, which carry the key step's destination disclosure and the config-rewrite notice) **plus** the new fields (`title`, `origin`, `stepIndex`, `stepCount`, `exitLabel`, `placement`, the stack depth). A test enumerates the snapshot's keys against the comparison and an explicit exclusion list, so a field added without a decision fails, and it is mutation-checked. The clock and any spinner are **not snapshot fields** ([ADR-0147](0147-a-system-started-question-is-a-sheet-with-a-frame-and-a-wait-clock.md) §2), `getSnapshot()` is reference-stable until an identity field changes, and a thousand ticks leave the acknowledgement valid. Each host has **one key history** shared by the popup and the chat store, so a key typed into a popup is seen by the guards of a card that opens next.
- **A popup that throws fails closed.** Each popup has an error boundary that closes it unanswered, clears its buffers and invalidates its acknowledgement; it does not end the Home through the renderer's error path, which would cancel a hosted run.
- **One active key reader at a time.** Sheets, popups, the tab host and the run view do not open their own `useInput`; keys reach them from the chat root, the Home root or the stand-alone native-prompt host. A popup adds no reader, and the gate that silences the chat's reader while a native prompt is active holds for it.
- **Late results.** Every asynchronous result carries a run id; a result for a closed or replaced run lands nowhere.
- **Notifications** are one channel owned by the popup step: transient, drawn on the hint row, queued while any chord or secret surface is shown, and the only way a closed popup or an instant command reports. It replaces the pattern that writes a notice to the Home and a note to the session.

### 3. Keys and answers

`↑/↓` select; `Enter` goes to the next step or applies; `Esc` goes back one step, clears a filter first and closes on the first step; `Ctrl+C` closes the popup (or first disarms an armed confirmation line) and arms no exit window ([ADR-0139](0139-a-workflow-run-is-hosted-inside-the-home.md) §5 is the precedent); neither confirms anything. Typed text goes only into a text field.

**How a step is answered.** A **settings** step applies with `Enter` because it is not an approval, and it first states its effect. A step that **writes a project file, widens authority through a persistent default (the startup mode), consents, spends or sends data elsewhere** keeps its **Ctrl-chord** answer and its guards; a preference that narrows nothing and consents to nothing (the default model for new chats) applies with `Enter` like any setting, and says where it applies. The inventory is [MVP-11 §4.3](../roadmap/phases/mvp-11.md#43-popups)'s table, and it is the contract, with these corrections:

- `/mode` **in a chat** applies on the last step after stating the effect (the keyboard already reaches `auto` in three presses of `Shift+Tab`). `/mode` **on the Home is a persistent write of the startup default**, so it keeps today's confirmation that defaults to *No* and is answered with `Ctrl+Y`, and the saved `auto` default keeps its extra admission. *(Refinement: the plan listed it as an `Enter` setting.)*
- `/models` applies on step 2 after stating where it applies; **when the provider changes, the scope is not pre-selected to "this chat"** (the conversation would go to another provider on an `Enter`), and the step says what goes: the text sent and bounded content-free tool receipts ([MVP-11 §5.5](../roadmap/phases/mvp-11.md#55-security-invariants-the-floor-that-does-not-move) rule 13). Within one provider the default is the chat.
- `/permissions` withdraws by `[d]` then `Enter` inside [ADR-0137](0137-permissions-is-a-sheet-and-esc-steps-back-out-of-the-full-view.md)'s window; a withdrawal that leaves a session approval in place keeps saying so **inside the popup**, not in a transient notification.
- `/providers` takes a masked key with an explicit *save anyway*; `/init`, `/import`, `/pricing`, `/export` and `/run` confirm with `Ctrl+Y`; the doctor's deep check arms with `[d]` and confirms with `Ctrl+Y`; `/resume` and the pickers select with `Enter`.
- **The first-run environment-key consent is a configuration write answered by `Enter`, which a popup that opens itself must not leave bare.** It is answered through the approval card's input guards (a dwell from the acknowledged frame, the quiet gap, the typing window that counts the composer's keys typed before the popup opened, and the lockout across the flow's steps), and **the opening guard** applies to every popup the system opens: keys that arrived before its first measured and flushed frame are dropped and counted as typing, nothing selects for about half a second after it appears, and an answer needs a key that arrived after it appeared. *(Refinement: the plan guarded only the doctor's `[d]`.)*

A popup adds no guard to an existing path and removes none; the doctor's confirmation reuses the existing 800 ms confirmation constant and the same dwell origin. A popup **opens on the current value**, so a bare `Enter` chain changes nothing.

### 4. Presentation

- **Alternate screen:** an absolutely positioned box in the overlay slot, centred, **as wide as the content column, `min(columns − 2 × margin, 76)`** (margin 6 for 80 to 99 columns and 8 from 100: 68 columns at 80×24, 76 from 88 columns up, and never wider), **at most 21 rows** for a standard popup (the inspection popup of §7 is the one exception and takes `rows − 1`). **Every popup row is filled with spaces to the frame's width**, which makes it opaque, and the spike showed that a row one cell short lets the body show through at its edge, so the padding is load-bearing. **Opacity is the safety property; the dimming is the look the maintainer asked for, and it is built to the same standard** *(ruled 2026-11-24, against the review's recommendation to ship opaque first)*. The renderer has no body-level dim, a dim wrapper must reach about 190 `Text` sites, and a bold span inside a dimmed one cancels the dim at its end (SGR 22 resets both), so the spike's plain-text rows hid the problem. Dimming is therefore built as a **leaf-level route**: one context-aware text wrapper through which every `Text` of the body and each markdown chunk passes, applied to the bold, inverse and coloured spans as well, and it is proved by a test that **every cell outside the popup carries the dim attribute on a body that contains bold, inverse and colour spans**. It is passed at paint time and does not enter the wrap cache's key. If the route cannot be made to pass that test in the popup step, the popup ships opaque and the maintainer is told, rather than shipping a dim that leaks.
- **Measure the content, not the box.** The acknowledgement is handed the popup's **content node, which has no fixed size**, so a row that wraps or an extra row grows the measured height and fails closed; measuring a fixed-size outer box would report the box's size however much spilled out of it. One geometry function feeds the frame and every page model that depends on it (§7).
- **Inline:** the inline renderer cannot draw over text already in the scrollback, so the popup is a **framed block that replaces the body** (as a native card does today), wearing the popup frame, with the same content, keys and way back. A popup over the body would leave an 11-row block on the Home, which the doctor does not fit. **The whole live region stays at most `rows − 1` lines at every render**, not only when the popup opens: the block's height is recomputed from the live terminal size and the other rows of the live region, with a hard clamp that falls back to the notice, because the renderer clears the screen and the user's scrollback and re-prints every static line once the live region reaches the terminal's height (the spike measured it in a real terminal: a region of 23 of 24 rows keeps the earlier output, one of 24 rows loses it when it next shrinks, which is the popup closing, and one of 25 wipes it while the popup is open; the renderer's own probe shows the same cliff, and the text re-sent grows with the session). The matrix asserts that no captured inline output contains the scrollback-clear sequence, and a test resizes while a popup is open (80×30, 80×25, 80×24).
- **Inline and the accessible mode:** an inline popup draws **rules without side bars** (a screen reader would voice a border glyph per line), has **no spinner and no ticking text**, and windows its lists to ten rows or fewer. It is part of the screen-reader smoke rows of the close-out step.
- **Below 80×24** the popup is replaced by a notice with the way back; a resize that would hide a pending chord step closes it unanswered and clears its buffers (a resize while a key is typed says `Resized · key cleared, paste it again`).
- **Renderer dependence.** The overlay relies on the renderer's absolute-position compositing, which nothing in the CLI used before; a test pins the behaviour across upgrades. It costs 0.4 to 1.6 ms a frame at 80×24 to 200×60, and any change rewrites the whole frame, so a popup adds no repaint source above 1 Hz.

### 5. States, and stacking

Every popup draws and tests four content states and its behaviour on a resize: *loading* (a **static** `…` and the elapsed seconds once past 5 s, refreshed at most once a second, never at the frame rate, and the cancel key it really has), *empty* (what is empty and what to do), *error* (what failed and the next action **inside** the TUI, never a shell command), and *overflow* (a windowed list with `▲ N more` / `▼ N more`, the highlighted row always in the window); *resize* is the rule of §4. A popup may open **one** other popup (the doctor's remedy opens the provider popup where one exists); the depth is two. A child is a new frame with **its own secret buffer, settle time and abort**, `Esc` on the child returns to its opener with the opener's state intact, and `Ctrl+C` closes the whole stack.

### 6. The residue rule, and its exception

A report or settings popup writes **nothing** into the conversation transcript and **nothing** into a Home notice. Confirmations are the status line, which shows the new value, plus a transient notification; errors stay in the popup. **The one exception** ([OD-1](../roadmap/phases/mvp-11.md#72-decisions-od-1-to-od-23)) is the **model switch marker** and its notice of unresolved external effects that [ADR-0059](0059-cli-mid-session-model-reseat.md) puts under the conversation when a live session is moved to another model, because it records where the conversation changed hands: it describes the conversation, not a setting.

A flow that **writes** (`/providers`, `/init`, `/import`, `/pricing`, `/export`) ends in a persistent *Done* step that names the target, project-relative, and closes with `Enter` or `Esc` (a timed notification is not evidence of a write). **A change that widens authority keeps a durable, content-free record** ([MVP-11 §5.5](../roadmap/phases/mvp-11.md#55-security-invariants-the-floor-that-does-not-move) rule 16 and OD-1, kept). Three of its four cases already have one, which the popups keep: the config file for a consent and for a startup default, and the grant store for a remembered command. **The fourth, a live session's change of mode into `accept-edits` or `auto`, has none today** (the design review found no table, no event and no persisted notice for it, and the key route, `Shift+Tab`, leaves nothing at all). So it gets one, in the settings step: a **content-free system row** appended to the session, in the manner of the interruption row of [ADR-0119](0119-an-interrupted-turn-stays-as-a-content-free-marker.md): no content, a closed pair of modes (`from`, `to`), never drawn in the transcript, never sent to the model, skipped when a resume rebuilds the context, and readable from the history. It needs **one additive migration and one field in the shared session schema**, which is the only data-model change of this MVP; no earlier reader has to be kept readable, since the product is not yet in use. The notification after **any** change into `auto` or `accept-edits`, by the popup, by `Shift+Tab` or by a typed argument, carries the one-line effect, so the key route does not skip the risk text the popup shows.

**A typed argument or a key applies directly, unless it would send, spend or make a persistent write**: `/mode plan`, `/effort high`, `/trim 20`, `Shift+Tab` and `Ctrl+T` change the session only, so they apply with the same result as the popup, open none, and (for a change into `accept-edits` or `auto`) leave the record above; `/doctor --deep` and `/cost --release` open the popup **at their confirmation step**. A command typed on a surface that does not offer it answers with one line that names the surface that has it.

### 7. The inspection popup (the full view and `/details`)

Pressing `[v]` on an approval opens its full view, today a screen that replaces the body ([ADR-0134](0134-the-full-view-and-the-tool-details-are-one-readable-inspection-screen.md)). It is drawn as a **large popup that belongs to the sheet** ([OD-17](../roadmap/phases/mvp-11.md#72-decisions-od-1-to-od-23), option B): `rows − 1` rows by `columns − 2` columns, **78 × 23 at 80×24**, because a standard popup (about 8 body rows) would give back the reading ADR-0134 won (the body was 9 rows before it, 14 to 17 after) and the large one keeps the body at 14 rows by putting the screen's name and origin on the popup's top edge, the identity and counts on one row and the closing note in the legend. The page is already projected from the geometry it is given, so the popup changes the numbers, not the model.

- **The approval sheet owns it.** Opening it does not suspend the sheet, no other popup may be drawn over it, and the sheet stays pending underneath with its guards running from their existing origins. **While it is open only its own host may mark a page as inspected, and the sheet underneath holds no custody**: the sheet's guards keep their time origins and run on, but its answer keys are inactive while the popup holds the keyboard, so an answer can only be given from the popup's own answer rows, on the popup's own acknowledged frame.
- **The geometry has one source.** One function feeds the popup frame, the page model, the required-target and identity computations and the copy, and the acknowledgement measures the popup's content node (§4). Two mutation tests keep it honest: a body row one cell too wide, and one extra row, must each refuse the acknowledgement and keep Allow locked. Otherwise a page fitted to one geometry and drawn in another could record rows the person never saw as inspected, which unlocks Allow for content that was off screen.
- **Keys and locks are unchanged.** The keys of [ADR-0134](0134-the-full-view-and-the-tool-details-are-one-readable-inspection-screen.md) §5 stay; **`Esc` and `[b]` step back to the approval and never reject** ([ADR-0137](0137-permissions-is-a-sheet-and-esc-steps-back-out-of-the-full-view.md) §4); `Ctrl+C` stops the turn **and arms the exit window here**, unlike in any other popup (where it only closes), and the legend says so. The answers stay Ctrl chords in the popup's two answer rows with the card's guards. Allow stays locked until the whole target has been on screen and every masked value has been revealed, the lock names its cause, and a hidden changed line earns no session-wide grant. Every row is fitted by the whole-row width measure and copy stays the masked companion of the exact page drawn. **A mashed `Esc` does not reject by accident**: the `Esc` that steps back from the popup to the approval starts the existing 400 ms settle, and an `Esc` that arrives inside it is ignored (it neither rejects nor stops the turn); one after it rejects as it does today. The guard only ever suppresses a rejection, never an approval, uses an injected clock and is pinned by a test; the sheets step's security sitting reads it.
- **The measured-frame acknowledgement covers the popup's frame**, so an answer counts only for the frame the user saw.
- **`/details`** opens the same container on the newest call, started by the user from a chat; `Enter` opens a call, `Esc` steps back to the list and then closes; no approval is pending there, so no answer rows are drawn. Inline, the same page is a framed block, which is what the inline full view already is.

*Considered:* **A** keep the screen and give it the popup's anatomy (the least risk, no overlay), **C** a standard-size popup (rejected for reading). B is chosen because it is what was asked and it keeps the reading; it is a rigor increment because it reframes the approval path.

### 8. `/doctor`, the model report

`/doctor` opens a report popup from the Home's palette, from a chat's palette and when typed; it never prints into the Home's notice or the transcript. A heading carries the dominant outcome and one row per check carries a glyph, a name, a status word and a short detail; the quick tier adds one row, **provider keys**, which warns at zero. That row **displays no key and sends nothing**; it reuses the availability computed at start and refreshes it between providers with a bounded `checking…` state, and never reads the keychain on the render path (a locked keychain or an access prompt blocks the event loop). Controls: `↑/↓` select a row, `Enter` shows its full detail and what to do (and, where a remedy exists on this surface, offers it as one more step), `[r]` runs the **quick** check again and keeps any deep rows (it is labelled for what it does), `[d]` arms the deep tier, `[c]` copies, `Esc` closes. Every label and detail is sanitised and glyph-blanked, and on this surface, which has a chord step, the **full scrub** applies ([ADR-0145](0145-the-cli-has-one-surface-language.md) §5): the rows show MCP ids from agent YAML, tool names and skip reasons from servers, and provider error details, all of it remote or repository text; a detail that can echo a provider's answer is also secret-scrubbed.

**The deep tier is the only control that leaves the machine, so it is a consent and not a keystroke** ([OD-11](../roadmap/phases/mvp-11.md#72-decisions-od-1-to-od-23)). `[d]` opens a confirmation line that says **what leaves and to whom, by the origin that will receive the key**, not by the provider's name (a stored custom endpoint changes where the key goes): `Sends one small request with your key to: <origins>. MCP servers are only reported, never contacted.` What the line lists is **frozen when it is armed**: the run is given exactly those provider ids **and the origins resolved for them at that moment**, and refuses any other id and any other origin, so neither a key stored in between nor an endpoint changed in between is contacted unlisted (a test changes a custom endpoint after arming and shows the request cannot go to it). The line is answered with **`Ctrl+Y`**, which counts only after the line was drawn, measured and flushed, at least 800 ms after **that frame was acknowledged** (not after the `[d]` press), within the existing confirmation window, and only if no non-navigation key arrived since the popup opened or since arming; `Esc`, `Ctrl+C` and any other key disarm it, a paste answers nothing, and `[d]` is ignored during the popup's settle time. A typed `/doctor --deep` opens the popup with the line already open and still needs the chord. **There is no new egress path** (the deep tier uses the existing provider seam and its custom-endpoint policy) and its MCP rows only report a session's already-connected servers and skipped tools. **One deep run can be in flight**, owned by the host with its own abort controller chained to the work owner's signal: **`Esc`, closing the popup and exiting abort it**, and the loading state's cancel key is real. A **completed** result is kept for the process and shown when `/doctor` is opened again, with its age. Tests: an injected hanging provider (reopening and pressing `[d]` three times sends one set of requests; `Esc` aborts at once), and, for MCP, a throwing proxy manager and transport, a spy on process spawning and a recording fetch whose origins must be a subset of the consent line. On the Home the deep tier is new behaviour (today the Home runs only the quick tier and says `--deep` is not accepted), and its remedy table names only surfaces that exist ([MVP-11 §4.4](../roadmap/phases/mvp-11.md#44-reports-and-settings-as-popups)).

### 9. The evidence, the conditions and the fallback

**What the spike showed** (2026-11-24; Ink 7.1.0 mounted with the Home's options, tmux 3.7b on macOS; [the kit](../roadmap/phases/mvp-11/spike/README.md) and [Step 1's account](../roadmap/phases/mvp-11.md#step-1--decisions-adrs-spike-and-baseline)):

- Alternate screen, 80×24, popup 68×21: 15 checks compare every captured screen cell with a model of the frame. The popup is opaque and its border straight; the 412 cells outside it are dimmed and none of its own; `Esc` leaves exactly the idle frame; a resize to 100×30 re-centres it with no leftovers; below 80×24 the notice replaces it; quitting restores the shell's text. The large 78×23 popup passes the same 15. A build without dimming fails only the dimming check; a build whose rows lack their padding fails six.
- **Occlusion is real and the bounds check cannot see it:** a later sibling is drawn on top of the popup (z-order is tree order) while the repository's `fitsTerminalBounds` still returns true; a comparison of the drawn frame with the popup's own rows catches it. The popup passes the repository's own bounds check and measured-frame acknowledgement, which reject a popup past the bottom or wider than its container. **A fixed-size popup box hides its own overflow from that check** (a probe measured the box as 78×23 while its content was 78×31), which is why §4 measures the content node.
- **Inline:** the popup draws in the flow above the prompt and repeated open and close leave no row and no copy in the scrollback, within the `rows − 1` rule of §4.
- **Glyph widths,** asked of the terminal with a cursor position report in tmux, the VS Code terminal (VS Code 1.147.0) and Terminal.app (build 488): all 37 glyphs of the table measure one column, as the renderer assumes. By hand in the VS Code terminal and in Terminal.app the maintainer reported the popup straight with no body text inside, the text behind it dimmed, `Esc` leaving no trace, `⚠` and `◉` not shifting the border and a short window showing the notice.
- **The design review's probes** (the renderer, a fake terminal): the overlay costs 0.4 to 1.6 ms a frame; a dimmed body breaks on a bold span inside it (the reason popups ship opaque); the inline scrollback clear is sent exactly when the live region reaches the terminal's height.

**What is not shown:** Windows Terminal, iTerm2, an SSH session, the inline form by hand in the two real terminals, terminals with the ambiguous-width setting on, focus behaviour, whether a terminal that reflows on resize leaves a stale chord legend in the scrollback, and the Home's own code path (the spike is a prototype app, not the Home). The two real terminals rest on the maintainer's observation.

**Conditions.** (1) **A chord or secret step may move into a popup only on evidence.** The evidence covers tmux (automated), the VS Code terminal and Terminal.app (by hand); chord steps (the deep tier, the run and write confirmations) proceed on it. **The maintainer ruled on 2026-11-24 that the key step of the first-run and provider popup (a secret) also moves into the popup without a Windows Terminal row, and accepted the risk:** the password field is not yet checked on Windows Terminal, iTerm2 or over SSH. Those rows are carried by MVP-10's Windows runbook and the close-out matrix, the security sitting of that step reads the field's buffer handling (cleared on close, resize and sheet arrival) with the missing rows in view, and a failing row later triggers the fallback below for that step. (2) The occlusion frame test of §2, the content-node measurement of §4 and the custody tests of §2 exist before any chord or secret popup ships. (3) Each row that is run later is recorded in a dated note here.

**The fallback ([OD-3](../roadmap/phases/mvp-11.md#72-decisions-od-1-to-od-23)).** If a row that is run later fails, the same anatomy is drawn as a **docked panel** (the popup's content in the sheet's place) and a centred overlay is kept only where the evidence passed. The steps and the cut line do not move.

### 10. What stays a screen, and two commands

The Home's tab host (`/workflows`, `/agents`, `/permissions`), the run view and `/help` on the Home stay screens: they are longer than a popup's 21 rows and they are places you work in, not steps you pass through ([OD-21](../roadmap/phases/mvp-11.md#72-decisions-od-1-to-od-23)); in a chat `/workflows`, `/permissions` and `/help` are popups (`/agents` exists only on the Home). **`/export` gets a one-step confirmation popup** that shows the target path and whether a file would be overwritten ([OD-10](../roadmap/phases/mvp-11.md#72-decisions-od-1-to-od-23)): a behaviour change in the safe direction, and a file write, so rigor. **`/compact` does not ask first**; it shows what it spent in a notification, and the question is revisited if the usability run shows surprise ([OD-22](../roadmap/phases/mvp-11.md#72-decisions-od-1-to-od-23)).

### Alternatives considered

- **A docked panel for everything** (the sheet's place). Kept as the fallback of §9, not the design: the maintainer asked for a popup, the Home has no sheet place the conversation would stay visible behind, and a docked panel shrinks the transcript for a task that is not about it.
- **A full-screen page per command.** Rejected: it hides the conversation and is heavy for a short list ([ADR-0137](0137-permissions-is-a-sheet-and-esc-steps-back-out-of-the-full-view.md) §1 rejected it for the same reason).
- **A centred modal for the approval.** Rejected ([ADR-0129](0129-the-approval-card-is-docked-bounded-and-says-only-what-matters.md)); the inspection popup belongs to the sheet and keeps it pending.
- **A popup that a system event may open.** Rejected, apart from the first-run exception and its opening guard: a popup that appears while the person types is the typing-ahead hazard the approval guards were built against.
- **Grow the prompt channel into the popup host.** Rejected: it is one promise per step, only some overlays run on it, and it is a 660-line closure; two drivers on one frame are smaller than one channel bent to both.
- **Restore a closed read-only report after a sheet.** Rejected: no definition of "restored" survives a popup that is mid-step.
- **Print the report into the conversation and add `Esc`.** Rejected: it is the residue the maintainer asked to remove.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Reports and settings stop polluting the conversation and the Home, and the user always sees how to leave (`Esc`).
- `/doctor` becomes a place to act (re-check, deep check, a row's remedy, copy) instead of a block that scrolls away.
- The approval's full view reads as a proper popup with every lock and guard of the approval intact and a geometry that fails closed.
- The doctor's network use becomes a stated, chord-confirmed consent that names the receiving origins and cannot widen after it is armed.
- A popup cannot silently take custody from a card, hide one, or be answered by what was typed before it appeared.

### Negative

- **The overlay depends on one renderer behaviour** that nothing used before, and it is built in a new directory with its own predicate, slot and tests. Mitigation: the pin test across upgrades, the occlusion frame test, the single absolute layer, and the docked-panel fallback stays designed.
- **The evidence is partial.** Windows Terminal, iTerm2 and SSH are not run, and the secret step moves into the popup with that accepted. Mitigation: the conditions of §9; the Windows rows join the runbook MVP-10 owes.
- **Two body drivers share one frame.** More structure than one channel. Mitigation: the frame is pure and shared, the drivers copy two existing patterns, and the controller and the chat host only route.
- **The dimming route touches about 190 `Text` sites in about 20 files** and a bold span can still leak the body through it. Mitigation: the leaf-level route and the every-cell test of §4, and the opaque fallback if the test cannot be satisfied.
- **An inline popup replaces the body** and is only as tall as the live region allows. Mitigation: recomputed at every render, clamped, and tested through a resize.
- **Every command that becomes a popup breaks its pins** (the picker, mode, effort, permissions and doctor suites). Mitigation: each increment lists the pins it broke on purpose; the existing secret tests are re-run inside the popup, not rewritten.
- **Persistent records of widened authority are not added.** The residue rule keeps today's records and no more. Mitigation: registered as its own decision.
